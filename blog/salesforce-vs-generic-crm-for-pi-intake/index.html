Salesforce vs. a Generic CRM for PI Intake: What Actually Matters

Most personal injury firms don't have a CRM problem, they have an intake architecture problem. Here's what actually determines whether a CRM helps or gets ignored.

TL;DR: Most PI firms already own a CRM, they just haven't wired their intake channels into it. Salesforce is usually the right foundation because it connects natively to everything, treats automation as core rather than bolted on, and scales across multiple offices, but buying any new platform without fixing what feeds it won't solve the underlying problem.

Almost every mid-sized personal injury firm we talk to already owns a CRM. Most of them barely use it for intake. The tool isn't the problem. The way it's wired into the firm's actual lead flow is.

The question isn't "which CRM." It's "what feeds it."

A CRM is only as good as what reaches it, and when it reaches it. We've seen firms running Salesforce that still lose leads to a shared inbox, because the web form was never actually connected to the CRM in the first place. Swapping platforms wouldn't fix that. Rebuilding the pipe would.

Before comparing tools, it's worth asking three questions about your current setup:

  • Does every channel, web form, phone, chat, after-hours voicemail, land in the same system automatically?
  • Is a lead qualified and routed within minutes, or does it sit until someone opens the queue?
  • Can leadership see, in real time, where a lead is stuck and why?

If the answer to any of those is no, the CRM you're evaluating won't fix it on its own.

Why we build on Salesforce specifically

When the plumbing above is missing, Salesforce is usually the right foundation to build it on, for a few practical reasons:

  • It's built to be wired to everything. Native integrations with calendars, telephony, and marketing tools mean the "every channel lands in one place" requirement is solvable without duct tape.
  • Automation is a first-class feature, not a bolt-on. Qualification logic and routing rules live inside the platform instead of a separate tool that has to stay in sync with it.
  • It scales with multiple offices. Firms with five to forty attorneys across several locations need routing rules that account for practice area, jurisdiction, and case load, and Salesforce handles that natively.

What generic CRMs usually get you instead

Lighter CRMs built for general sales teams tend to assume a slower, more manual sales motion; something closer to weeks of nurturing than minutes of triage. They're often fine for outbound business development. They're rarely built for the specific problem of triaging a high volume of inbound, time-sensitive, multi-channel leads the way PI intake requires.

The real fix is rarely "buy a new tool"

In almost every engagement we run, the Pilot phase is less about replacing software and more about connecting what a firm already has: web forms, phone systems, calendars, and Salesforce, into one pathway with AI qualification in the middle. No new hires. Same ad spend. A system that actually catches what's already coming in.
