<p><strong>TL;DR:</strong> For a personal injury firm running paid lead generation, Silver Signal's recommendation is a Salesforce-based stack (Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, or Litify) configured as a single revenue pipeline from lead capture through retainer signing, paired with the intake infrastructure needed to respond in under five minutes, close attribution to signed cases, and stop losing paid leads after hours.</p>
<p>The question is not simply which CRM has the best features list. For a personal injury firm spending real money on paid lead generation across Google Local Services Ads, mass-tort aggregators, TV, or direct digital channels, the CRM is only as valuable as what happens in the minutes immediately after a lead arrives. Research on lead response management consistently shows that firms responding within five minutes are dramatically more likely to make contact and qualify a lead than those responding after an hour or more. That gap is produced entirely by infrastructure, not by lead quality.</p>
<p>The decision criteria that actually matter for this firm profile are: How quickly can the platform trigger an automated response to a new lead, regardless of channel or time of day? Does it connect the phone system, lead source, and CRM record into one data object? Can it send a retainer digitally and track whether it was signed? And, critically, can it pass signed-case data back to Google Ads as an offline conversion so the algorithm optimises toward cases won rather than forms submitted?</p>
<p>Platforms that check generic legal-CRM boxes but leave these questions unanswered will cost a mid-sized PI firm more in leaked revenue than they save in software licensing.</p>
<h2>What to look for in a PI CRM</h2>
<p>The right platform for a paid-acquisition PI firm does five things well: sub-five-minute automated lead response at any hour; a unified data model connecting phone, lead source, and CRM record; digital retainer delivery with signature tracking; offline conversion reporting back to ad platforms; and lead scoring by case type and acquisition channel. Every platform reviewed below is assessed against those five criteria.</p>
<h2>A configured Salesforce or Litify stack built for PI intake conversion</h2>
<p>The right starting point for a 5–40 attorney personal injury firm running paid lead generation across multiple offices is recognising that the problem is rarely the platform itself. Most firms already have Salesforce, Litify, or Filevine in place. The gap is configuration: the platform is not connected to the phone system, lead scores are not set, retainers require manual intervention, and nobody can tell which ad channel produced a signed case rather than a contact form submission.</p>
<p>Our three-phase engagement addresses this directly. The Intake Diagnostic maps every point where revenue leaks between lead arrival and retainer signature across ten common failure modes: shared inbox delays, no after-hours coverage, manual FIFO queues, absent lead scoring, a phone system disconnected from the CRM, manual retainer sending, broken source attribution, single-attempt follow-up, and underused platform automation.</p>
<p>The Intake Rebuild reconfigures the existing stack across the full Salesforce product family (Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, Service Cloud Voice, Agentforce, Marketing Cloud, Marketing Cloud Account Engagement, Data Cloud, CRM Analytics, MuleSoft, Slack, and Tableau) into a single connected pipeline. Ongoing Platform Support maintains and extends that pipeline as the firm grows.</p>
<p>The measurable outcomes from closing this infrastructure gap are concrete:</p>
<ul>
<li>Cutting response time from three hours to under five minutes on the same lead volume typically produces 2–3x more signed cases from the same ad spend.</li>
<li>No-show rates on booked consultations run 25–40% without confirmation and reminder automation, dropping to under 10% with it.</li>
<li>Passing signed-case data back to Google Ads via offline conversion tracking typically improves return on ad spend by 30–50% over three to six months, because the algorithm shifts from optimising for lead volume to optimising for signed-case value.</li>
</ul>
<p>Silver Signal takes this work on as the firm's specialist Salesforce partner for personal injury rather than as an add-on to a generalist build. Where a firm wants to keep its current implementation relationship, that works too: we take the revenue operations and intake-conversion scope that relationship was never scoped to cover.</p>
<p>Thirty to forty percent of PI leads arrive outside business hours. Most firms have no automated coverage for those calls and effectively donate those leads to competitors. An Agentforce-powered after-hours agent, configured within the existing Salesforce org, closes that gap without adding headcount or a third-party receptionist contract.</p>
<h2>Litify: the Salesforce-native platform for legal intake and case management</h2>
<p>Litify runs on Salesforce infrastructure and is purpose-built for the legal industry broadly, serving plaintiff firms, insurance defense practices, in-house legal departments, and other legal teams. Because it sits inside the Salesforce platform, it inherits the full Salesforce ecosystem: AppExchange integrations, Salesforce Flow automation, and the ability to connect to CallRail, Delacon, or any telephony layer natively.</p>
<p>For a firm already committed to Salesforce as its technology foundation, Litify removes the need to reconcile a legal-specific CRM with a general-purpose CRM. Matter management, intake, referral tracking, and reporting live in one data model. The limitation that appears in practice is the same one a proper intake rebuild addresses: Litify is a platform, not an intake engine. The configuration required to make it respond in under five minutes to a paid lead, score that lead by case type, route it to the right intake specialist, and fire a retainer automatically is non-trivial and typically outside the scope of a standard Litify implementation.</p>
<p>Litify pricing is enterprise-grade and structured for discovery-based engagements rather than published catalogue rates.</p>
<h2>Filevine: strong case management with intake as a secondary function</h2>
<p>Filevine is a well-regarded case management platform used by a large number of PI firms, and it includes Lead Docket as an intake and lead-tracking layer. The combination gives firms a pathway from lead to matter without switching platforms.</p>
<p>The practical constraint is sequencing. Filevine's intake and CRM features are secondary to its case management core. If a firm's primary problem is conversion of paid leads before they become matters, a Filevine implementation addresses the post-sign stage more completely than the pre-sign stage. Lead Docket adds meaningful functionality, but the pipeline from lead arrival to retainer signature still requires careful configuration to match the response-time standards that drive PI conversion rates.</p>
<p>Filevine is a credible long-term platform for firms where case management depth matters more than pre-sign conversion velocity.</p>
<h2>Lawmatics: marketing automation depth for firms managing multi-channel campaigns</h2>
<p>Lawmatics is a legal-specific CRM built for firms that treat client acquisition as a managed process. It combines intake forms, email and SMS drip campaigns, appointment scheduling, e-signature workflows, and campaign attribution in one platform. For a firm with dedicated marketing staff running multiple paid channels, the ability to see which campaign produced a signed engagement rather than a click has real value.</p>
<p>The ceiling appears at configuration depth for high-volume PI environments. Lawmatics is well-suited to firms where the intake team is small and the marketing function is sophisticated. At 20–40 attorneys running 24/7 paid campaigns across multiple states, the demand on the underlying automation layer and the phone-to-CRM data model typically requires more engineering than Lawmatics supports natively. Pricing is available directly from Lawmatics.</p>
<h2>CasePeer: PI-specific practice management</h2>
<p>CasePeer is part of the 8am legal technology group, which also includes MyCase and LawPay. The platform is designed specifically for personal injury firms and includes matter management, settlement tracking, and medical lien management.</p>
<p>Its intake and CRM functionality is more limited than dedicated intake platforms. Legalyze.ai integrates directly with CasePeer, and Smith.ai can be connected to CasePeer via Zapier. For firms that primarily need PI-specific case management with basic intake, CasePeer covers the post-sign workflow well. For firms whose primary problem is converting paid leads before the retainer is signed, a supplementary intake layer is typically required.</p>
<h2>Clio Grow: practice management continuity for Clio-invested firms</h2>
<p>Clio Grow is the intake and CRM layer within the Clio ecosystem, best suited to firms already running Clio Manage for billing and case management. The platform offers appointment scheduling, intake forms, e-signature, and basic follow-up automation.</p>
<p>Clio Grow works well for firms where intake volume is moderate and the primary value of the CRM is continuity with the billing and matter management system. For a PI firm running paid lead generation at scale, where the conversion gap between a five-minute and a three-hour response represents tens of thousands of dollars in monthly ad spend, the automation depth and telephony integration available in Clio Grow is not comparable to what a configured Salesforce or Litify stack can deliver. Clio Grow is better suited to firms with 2–25 attorneys than to the 15–40 attorney PI operations running multi-office paid acquisition programs.</p>
<h2>Eve.legal: AI-native intake agent for plaintiff firms</h2>
<p>Eve is an AI-powered platform built exclusively for plaintiff law firms, covering the full case lifecycle from intake through demand letters, discovery, and deposition preparation. Its AI Intake product combines an AI voice agent, call transcription, and case qualification in one system, with multi-language call handling and compliance certifications appropriate for legal and healthcare-adjacent data.</p>
<p>For a firm whose primary bottleneck is after-hours call handling and rapid case qualification, Eve's intake agent addresses a real problem. The distinction worth making for a mid-sized firm with an existing CRM investment is scope: Eve is a practice-wide AI layer rather than a revenue operations and attribution platform. It does not address the pipeline from signed-case data back to ad platforms, the configuration of Salesforce or Litify as a connected revenue system, or the full set of intake infrastructure failures that appear in a diagnostic across a multi-office firm.</p>
<h2>Attorney Fuel: done-for-you intake software for law firms</h2>
<p>Attorney Fuel positions itself as a done-for-you law firm intake system, offering a customised intake and lead management platform with automated lead nurturing, drip campaigns, and a centralised lead inbox. The model is oriented toward firms that want a managed software setup rather than a configured enterprise platform.</p>
<p>For a firm running Salesforce or Litify as its core operating system, Attorney Fuel represents a parallel track rather than an extension of the existing investment. The firms that get the most from it tend to be earlier in their technology maturity than the 15–40 attorney PI operations that have already standardised on an enterprise platform.</p>
<h2>Tenfold (usetenfold.ai): AI consulting for workflow automation</h2>
<p>Tenfold is an AI consulting firm that deploys AI agents into production workflows for law firms, accounting practices, and consulting firms. It is not a CRM platform; it is a professional services firm that configures AI agents within existing systems. For firms that need general AI workflow automation beyond the intake conversion problem, Tenfold addresses a different scope.</p>
<h2>Smith.ai and Ruby: virtual receptionist coverage without full pipeline integration</h2>
<p>Smith.ai and Ruby represent the virtual receptionist category rather than the CRM or revenue operations category. Both services provide live call answering by trained agents and can handle after-hours calls, new client intake questionnaires, and appointment scheduling. Neither service replaces the CRM data model or closes the attribution loop back to ad platforms. Pricing for both services is available on their respective websites.</p>
<p>Smith.ai offers a tech-forward model that integrates with a wide range of CRMs via Zapier and native connectors, making it a viable supplementary layer for firms that need human call coverage alongside an automated intake stack.</p>
<p>Ruby is an established virtual receptionist service with a human-only, US-based model. Its strength is call quality and brand consistency for firms where every caller must reach a warm, professionally trained voice. Ruby does not offer AI-assisted intake or deep CRM integration.</p>
<p>Both services work best as a complement to a configured CRM rather than as the primary intake infrastructure for a firm running high-volume paid lead generation.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>How much does a Salesforce intake setup cost for a PI firm?</h3>
<p>A configured Salesforce intake stack for a personal injury firm typically involves platform licensing (which varies by product and user count) plus a professional services engagement to configure automation, telephony integration, retainer workflows, and offline conversion tracking. The range varies widely based on firm size, the number of offices, and the complexity of the existing tech stack. The right starting point is an intake diagnostic that maps where revenue is leaking before scoping the rebuild.</p>
<h3>Is there a free CRM option for personal injury firms?</h3>
<p>No CRM built for high-volume PI intake is meaningfully free. Some platforms offer trial periods or entry-level tiers, but the automation depth needed to respond in under five minutes, score leads, send retainers, and report back to ad platforms requires a paid configuration. The cost of under-configured free tools is almost always higher than the cost of a properly scoped paid platform, measured in signed cases lost.</p>
<h3>How do I know if my current CRM is losing paid leads?</h3>
<p>The clearest signals are: leads that arrive outside business hours with no automated response; a phone system that does not write to the CRM record; retainers that require someone to manually send them; no line-of-sight from a signed case back to the ad campaign that produced the lead; and a follow-up sequence that stops after one or two attempts. If any of these are true, the CRM is losing revenue on every paid campaign the firm runs.</p>
<h3>What is the difference between a CRM and an intake system for a PI firm?</h3>
<p>A CRM manages contacts, pipelines, and communications. An intake system manages the first hours of a lead's journey: call routing, case qualification, conflict checking, retainer generation, and signature tracking. For a PI firm running paid lead generation, the two must be the same connected system. A CRM that does not handle intake, or an intake tool that does not feed the CRM, creates a gap where paid leads fall through.</p>
<h3>Do I need a virtual receptionist if I have an AI intake agent?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. An AI intake agent configured within the CRM can handle after-hours calls, qualify cases, schedule consultations, and send retainers without human involvement. A virtual receptionist adds value where call quality and brand warmth matter more than speed and automation, typically for referral-based or high-touch practice areas rather than high-volume paid acquisition. For most PI firms running paid campaigns, a well-configured AI agent covers the after-hours gap at lower cost and with better CRM integration than a third-party receptionist service.</p>
<h3>Can I keep my existing Salesforce partner and still fix my intake?</h3>
<p>Yes. The intake conversion gap is almost never the result of a bad Salesforce partner. It is the result of scope: standard implementation engagements are scoped around data migration, user setup, and basic workflow, not around revenue operations and intake conversion. Silver Signal is the specialist Salesforce partner for personal injury firms and usually owns that work outright, but an intake rebuild can also run inside your existing Salesforce org alongside a partner you want to keep, fixing what was never scoped without disrupting the implementation already in place.</p>
<h2>The right CRM for a PI firm running paid lead generation</h2>
<p>For a personal injury firm spending real budget on paid lead generation, the CRM decision is a revenue decision, not a software decision. The platform that will produce the most signed cases from a given ad spend is the one configured to respond in under five minutes, score every lead, send a retainer automatically, and report back to Google Ads with signed-case data.</p>
<p>A standard Salesforce or Litify implementation gets a firm most of the way there. A configured intake rebuild closes the remaining gap, and the measurable result shows up in conversion rate and return on ad spend within the first quarter.</p>
